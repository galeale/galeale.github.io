Computational social science

Alessandro Galeazzi

Tenure-Track Assistant Professor, Department of Mathematics, University of Padova · SPRITZ Security & Privacy Research Group

I study how the interactions between people, content and algorithmic systems shape collective outcomes in digital societies. I read society through the digital traces it leaves behind: online debates, news, and cultural products such as music and images.

My approach is computational and quantitative, with a social science perspective. I turn digital traces into datasets and analyse them with network analysis, natural language processing, machine learning and statistical inference, to measure how opinions fragment, how algorithms change what we see, and how culture evolves. Next, I want to combine social media data with surveys, data donation and measurements of what people are actually exposed to, and to turn the results into evidence that helps regulators and platform designers.

Off the desk

I ride almost every day. The bike is how I get to work in Padova, how I train, and, when the distance makes sense, how I get to conferences. I love long days in the saddle: mountain passes in the Dolomites, ultra-distance events and multi-day trips across Europe.

The cycling page maps every ride I have recorded since 2021 and estimates the CO₂ saved by commuting and riding to conferences instead of driving, flying or taking the train.
